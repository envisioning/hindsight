# Hype Cycle, 1995 to 2002: text evidence (unverified)

> Update 2026-09-27: full charts for every year from 1995 to 2002 were found and read into `1995.json` to `2002.json`. Sources are listed in INDEX.md. This file now serves as a cross-check. See "Checks against the charts" at the end.

Collected 2026-09-27 from public text sources. No full entry list was found for any edition from 1995 to 2002. Everything below is partial and must be checked against the cited pages before it becomes a claim. Labels from cio.de and Out-Law are summaries of German and English press text, not verbatim.

## Do the editions exist?

- Jackie Fenn (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/first-hype-cycle-origin-story-jackie-fenn): the first chart was in the February 1995 note "When to Leap on the Hype Cycle", with "a handful of technologies plotted". Clients asked for an update in 1996. By 2003 Gartner ran an annual special report. She does not confirm an edition for each year from 1997 to 2002.
- Michael Mullany discarded "1995 through 1999" from his analysis, which implies charts exist for those years.
- Gartner document titles found in search results (gartner.com returns 403, so not read): "2000 Hype Cycle of Emerging Technologies" (doc 302322); "2001 Hype Cycle of Emerging Trends and Technologies" (doc 334359, 2 July 2001); "Gartner's 2002 Hype Cycle for Emerging Technologies" (doc 358842, 28 May 2002); "2002 Emerging Technologies Hype Cycle: Trough to Plateau" (doc 358349).

## Checks against the charts (2026-09-27)

- 1995: the chart has 10 labels. Mullany's "Emergent Computing" is "Emergent Computation". Speech Recognition is on the slope (Tempo is right; Mullany's "climbing the Plateau" is not). Virtual Reality is on the descending side, as TechViz says. Wireless Communications is on the rising side, at about half height, not near the peak.
- 1996: Java, Internet Terminals and World Wide Web are at the peak, as Fenn says.
- 1997 and 1998: nothing to check; no text source was found before the charts.
- 2000: Gartner's summary (WAP/Wireless Web, ASPs, Webtops at the peak; biometrics and voice portals rising) matches the chart. Bluetooth sits just past the peak on the descending side, which explains the Visual Capitalist and Capitalogix lists. Jini, micropayments and Java Language are on the chart; Synthetic Characters is on the rising side.
- 2001: Gartner's summary (Web Services, m-commerce, enterprise IM, PDA phones, digital signatures at the peak) matches the chart. Head Mounted Displays is on the rising side.
- 2002: every item from cio.de and Out-Law is on the chart with the same phase. "Public Authentication Services" (Mullany) is not on the chart; the chart has "Public key infrastructure". Personal fuel cells has a 5-10 marker, as cio.de says.
