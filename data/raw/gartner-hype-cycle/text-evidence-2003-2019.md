# Hype Cycle text evidence, 2003 to 2019

Collected 2026-09-27 by research agents through web search and summarized web fetches. Not verbatim: check each item against its source before it becomes a claim. Editions 2005 to 2017 are covered in full by `2005.json` to `2017.json`; for those years, the sources below are cross-checks only. 2018 and 2019 are in `2018.json` and `2019.json`.

## Michael Mullany's spreadsheet (2000 to 2016)

https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1NkC0g60q-6w72nksayvdfzCT5oOmBy97XBCGw-tW1p8 (linked from "8 Lessons from 20 Years of Hype Cycles", 2016). 204 technology rows, one column per year. Each cell is 0 (absent) or his own position code, 1 (earliest) to 8 (mature). The codes are not Gartner phases or bands, and he merged labels. Cross-check only.

## 2004 (Gartner says 37 technologies): now complete in `2004.json`

Source: https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2750155/welche-technologien-wichtig-werden.html (German, quoting Gartner analyst Linden).

- Peak: WiMAX; Ultrawideband (UWB); inkjet-based manufacturing; Unified Communications.
- Trough: WiFi/hotspots; Tablet PCs; XBRL.
- Slope: location-aware services; VoIP.
- Plateau: internal Web services; speech recognition for telephony and call centres; instant messaging.
- Band only: Wikis 2-5; molecular transistors >10.
- Named without phase: internal supercomputer grids; protein-DNA logic; RFID on pallets and cartons; Linux on the desktop.
- Mullany lists 31 entries for 2004.
