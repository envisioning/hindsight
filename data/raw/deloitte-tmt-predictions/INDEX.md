# Deloitte TMT Predictions: collected editions

Facts only: prediction headline, a short quote with the key number, target year, metric and value where stated, subject label, source. Body text, charts and figures are not stored (see NOTICE.md). Nothing here is graded.

## Verify-first answers

- **Series.** Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Limited (Deloitte Global), Technology, Media & Telecommunications industry group. Annual. From 2011 one report, "Technology, Media & Telecommunications Predictions <year>". From 2005 to 2010, separate Technology, Media and Telecommunications reports (2005: four, telecoms split into Mobile and Wireless and Wireline), under the banner "TMT Trends: Predictions" to 2009; 2002 to 2004 mobile and telecoms only (see below). The 2011 foreword says 2011 is "for the first time being published as a single report, rather than three separate ones".
- **First edition.** Deloitte states the series "started in 2001 with ten predictions about mobile telephony" (Deloitte TMT Predictions collection page, archived). Deloitte's own edition counts fix the first edition as the one for 2002: 2009 is "the eighth year", 2011 "the 10th edition", 2012 "the eleventh year", 2019 "its 18th year", 2020 "the 19th edition". So the first edition was written in 2001 and covers 2002. The Internet Archive retry (2026-10-02) found it: Deloitte Consulting's four-page "m-Predictions: the top mobile Hot Topics for 2002" with ten numbered mobile predictions (dc.com, captured 2002-01-29). 2003 was again a Deloitte Consulting mobile "mPredictions" (known only from its 9 January 2003 press release). 2004 was two Deloitte Research TMT reports, Mobile and Wireless Predictions and Wire-line Predictions. Computerworld (20 January 2005) calls the 2005 Technology report "the company's first publication of technological predictions", which fits this mobile/telecom-only start.
- **Report structure by year.** 2002 and 2003: one mobile report. 2004: Mobile and Wireless, Wireline. 2005: four reports (Technology, Media, Mobile and Wireless, Wireline; ten predictions each). 2006 to 2009: Technology, Media, Telecommunications, ten chapters each. 2010: Technology 7, Media 8, Telecommunications 7. From 2011: one report.
- **Edition naming.** One file per edition year (the year the predictions are for). From the 2018 edition, Deloitte launches in the previous November or December; `published` records that.
- **Archive availability.** 2011 to 2018: full PDFs on deloitte.com (`/content/dam/assets-shared/legacy/docs/perspectives/2022/`). 2019: PDF on deloitte.com/content/dam/insights. 2020 to 2024: the Deloitte Insights edition URL (`.../technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/<year>.html`) serves the full report PDF. 2025 and 2026: Deloitte Insights web pages. 2005 to 2010: first read from Mondaq republications (Deloitte-contributed articles, often split into parts, some behind registration) and one Media 2010 PDF copy on hotnews.ro, while the Internet Archive was offline (2026-09-28). The retry (2026-10-02) found every missing 2004 to 2010 report as a PDF on archive.org: pre-2009 files under `deloitte.com/dtt/cda/doc/content/`, 2009 and 2010 files under `deloitte.com/assets/` (Dcom-Global, Dcom-Shared Assets, or a member-firm copy of the global English file: Kazakhstan for Technology 2008, Portugal for Telecommunications 2009, Canada for Telecommunications 2010). 2002 and 2003 are on `dc.com` (Deloitte Consulting). Found with the Wayback CDX API (`url=deloitte.com/dtt/&matchType=prefix&filter=mimetype:application/pdf`, `url=deloitte.com/assets/&matchType=prefix`, `url=dc.com&matchType=domain&filter=original:.*redict.*`). New entries cite the Wayback URL with `#page=`. Earlier entries keep their Mondaq source; their ranks number within the Mondaq part, appended entries use the chapter number in the full report.

## Deloitte's self-assessment (Deloitte's own claims, not Hindsight's grade)

- **Stated policy.** The 2011 and 2013 forewords: "We aim to provide clear Predictions endpoints, so that our accuracy can be evaluated annually."
- **2014 report card.** Deloitte's collection page: "2014's TMT Predictions hit the mark with 13 of the 14 predictions hitting a more than 70% accuracy rate." A "TMT Predictions report card" page existed (`www2.deloitte.com/bg/en/pages/technology-media-and-telecommunications/articles/tmt-predictions-report-card.html`); its content could not be read (live URL redirects, archive.org offline).
- **2013 midyear checkpoint.** An infographic and podcast reviewed the 2013 predictions at midyear (`gx-tmt-predictions2013-midyear-infographic.pdf`). Not read.
- **2025 "Updates" section.** "Updates: Past TMT Predictions' greatest hits and (near) misses" (https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2025/past-tmt-predictions-hits-and-misses.html) revisits seven past topics. Deloitte's summary: "We did well with past predictions, but nobody bats 1.000" and "(Spoiler: We did really well!)". Its own verdicts: 2021 Open RAN doubling from 35 to 70 "was too optimistic" (45 by March 2024); 2023 5G SA doubling to at least 200 operators "has not happened" (49 by March 2024); 2022 FWA growth "roughly in line"; 2024 women's elite sports over US$1 billion and 2022 RISC-V near US$1 billion by 2024 restated without a verdict. Values in `realized.json`.
- **2026 overview.** Deloitte notes its 2024 D2D satellite spending figure of US$3 billion came in at "around $4 billion".

## Entry fields

`rank` (order in the report; restarts per section in the three-report years), `section` (Technology / Media / Telecommunications to 2016 and 2005-2010; Mobile in 2002 and 2003; Mobile and Wireless / Wireline in 2004 and 2005; Deloitte's theme groups in 2022 to 2025; null otherwise), `title` (chapter headline), `quote` (the prediction sentence with its number, max 400 chars; "..." marks an omission), `target_year`, `metric`, `value` (as a string, with `>`, `<`, `~`, ranges), `unit`, `subject` (Hindsight's plain label), `source_url`, `confidence`, `note`.

Confidence: `high` = read from the Deloitte report text; `medium` = Deloitte text via a republication, or quote reassembled from a two-column PDF; `low` = summary-level statement, headline only, or a reporter's paraphrase.

## Open problems

- **2003 report not found.** Only the press release of 9 January 2003 was found. Searched: Wayback CDX for dc.com (all PDFs 2002 to 2004; every URL containing 'redict'), deloitte.com/dtt/ (all PDFs; URLs containing 'redict' to 2004), guessed file names (`m-predictions2003.pdf`, `mpredictions2003.pdf`, `m-predictions03.pdf`), and one web search.
- **2006 Technology chapters 1, 2, 5, 6** are represented by the earlier Mondaq executive-summary statements, not by chapter headlines (not re-captured, to avoid double claims).
- **2002 identification.** The 2002 paper does not use the TMT Predictions name; it is matched to Deloitte's description of the first edition (ten mobile predictions written in 2001). 2002 and 2003 come from Deloitte Consulting, 2004 from Deloitte Research TMT, 2005 on from the DTT TMT Industry Group.
- **Pre-2011 wording is soft.** Before 2010 the reports rarely say "Deloitte predicts"; claims use "may", "likely", "could". Several of these entries have no number and some are framings, not dated predictions (confidence `low`).
- **Sub-predictions not captured.** Chapters often carry several numeric claims (2016 US TV lists six; 2019 TV sports lists about fifteen). Only the headline claim per chapter is captured, with extra numbers in `note` where useful.
- **Publication days.** Most editions give only a month (January launch to 2017; November/December from the 2018 edition). Exact days confirmed only for 2014, 2020, 2021, 2025, 2026.
- **Revisions (not yet mapped to Subject ids).** Recurring subjects that may be `Revision` candidates: LEO satellite broadband (2020, 2023, 2024 D2D, 2026); Open RAN (2021, 2025 update); 5G standalone (2023, 2025 update); FWA (2022, 2025 update); women's sports (2021, 2024, 2025 update); RISC-V (2022, 2025 update); linear TV share (2010, 2011, 2012, 2022); VR (2016, 2021, 2023); 3D printing (2012, 2015, 2019); edge computing (2021, 2023); AI chips (2017, 2018, 2020, 2024); "The connectivity chasms deepen" title reused (2007 Telecommunications, 2015).
- **Source issues noticed.** The 2011 PDF running footer misspells "Talecommunications". The 2020 audiobook figure differs between report (US$3.5 billion) and a press summary seen via a fetch tool (US$5 billion); the report figure is used.
