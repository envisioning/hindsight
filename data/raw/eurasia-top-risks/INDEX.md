# Eurasia Group Top Risks: collected editions

Facts only: ranking, rank, label as printed, a one-sentence summary in Hindsight's words, the headline sentence as `quote` (max 400 characters), source. No report text beyond that (see NOTICE.md). Nothing here is graded.

## Verified first

- **First public edition found: 2007** ("Top 7 Political Risks for 2007", outlined by Ian Bremmer on 2 January 2007, with PDF and red herrings). This is the earliest edition whose content is on the web.
- **2006 existed but is missing.** Eurasia Group's own press-coverage page (archived 2006) lists, on 3 January 2006, a blog item on "Eurasia Group's forecasts for the top political risks of 2006". The list itself was not found: no Eurasia Group page, PDF or press release for 2006 is in the Wayback Machine index, and the blog was not linked.
- **No evidence for 2005 or earlier.** The archived press listings for 2002-2005 and the homepages for January 2004, 2005 and 2006 do not mention Top Risks. A "2005 Q&A" note (10 January 2005) exists but is a question-and-answer outlook, not a ranked risk list; it is not captured. Eurasia Group was founded in 1998, and the 2008 and 2009 press releases call Top Risks "annual", so a client-only list before 2006 cannot be ruled out.
- **Self-grades.** Eurasia Group does not publish a score or letter grade. Year-end prose reviews exist for 2022, 2024 and 2025 (GZERO Media, Eurasia Group's media company) and a video for 2018. See `self_assessment.json`. These are their claims, not Hindsight's grades.

## Method notes

| Years | List | Red herrings | Notes |

| 2008 | 9 risks | 6 | "Top 9" |
| 2009-2026 | 10 risks | 3-6 | 2013, 2014 add an unranked "? - North Korea"; 2019 adds an unranked "*Brexit" (captured as `wildcard`, rank null) |

- Every risk is for the calendar year of the edition (`horizon: "calendar year"`).
- Red herrings are not ranked; `rank` is the printed order.
- Authors: 2007-2009 Ian Bremmer; 2010-2011 Ian Bremmer and David Gordon; 2012 and 2014 no byline on the archived page; 2013 first person by the founder; 2015-2026 Ian Bremmer and Cliff Kupchan.
- `summary` is Hindsight's paraphrase. `quote` is the headline sentence printed under the risk on the landing page. Where no headline exists, it is the first sentence of the risk section, and the entry `note` says so.

## Sources used

- Current landing pages `eurasiagroup.net/issues/top-risks-YYYY` (2016-2026; 2019 is `/issues/top-risks-for-2019`). 2012-2015 and 2019 at `/issues/top-risks-YYYY` return 404; older pages lived at `/pages/top-risks-YYYY` and are read from the Wayback Machine.
- PDFs read: 2007, 2009 (archived), 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020 (original), 2023. 2021 and 2022 PDFs returned HTTP 429 (rate limit). 2024-2026 PDFs are behind an email form and were not downloaded.
- Red-herrings pages: `eurasiagroup.net/live-post/...red-herrings...` for 2021-2026.
- Press releases (archived) for 2008 and 2009.
- Self-assessments: gzeromedia.com (see `self_assessment.json`).
- Web search was unavailable during capture (session limit). Discovery used the Wayback Machine URL index for eurasiagroup.net and gzeromedia.com.

## Open problems and source oddities

1. **Labels differ between channels.** 2008 #1 is "US" on the publications page and "US anti-globalization" in the press release. 2009 #1 is "Congress" in the note and "US financial regulation and the rise of Congress" in the press release. 2007 #7 is "Afghanistan/Pakistan" on the page, "Pakistan/Afghanistan" in the PDF. 2007 long-term risk (a) is "Pandemic influenza" on the page, "Avian influenza" in the PDF. 2010 #8 heading adds "(no, not Afghanistan)". 2017 #7 "vs" vs "versus". Each file keeps one label and notes the other.
2. **Capitalisation.** 2020 and 2021 landing pages print labels in capitals. 2020 labels follow the original PDF contents page; 2021 labels are case-normalised (PDF not read).
3. **2020 has two versions.** Original 6 January 2020; "Coronavirus Edition" 19 March 2020. Ranking and labels match; texts were not compared risk by risk.
4. **Red herrings lists differ between summary and detail.** 2022 landing page names "Cold War 2.0, Brazil, Migration"; the red-herrings page also has a "Taiwan" heading. 2026 landing page calls US-China conflict a red herring, but the red-herrings page does not list it separately.
5. **2008 red herrings** come from the press release list. The red-herrings PDF is archived but damaged.
6. **Headline quotes for 2009** come from the press release, not the PDF; for 2010-2015 they are first sentences of the risk section; for 2012 the printed subtitles (for example "The Muddle is the Risk").
7. **Publication days** are exact where printed (2007-2009, 2015-2026). 2010-2014 are month only.
8. **Source content worth a later look:** the 2016 red herring "US elections" states that Trump could not be the Republican nominee or beat Clinton (PDF red-herrings section). The 2022 self-review says Eurasia Group did not expect a full invasion of Ukraine (risk 5, "Russia").
9. **Quote spacing.** 2016 and 2007 quotes taken from PDFs have word spacing and hyphenation repaired from text extraction.
