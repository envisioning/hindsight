# Gartner Top Strategic Predictions: collected editions

Facts only: prediction headline (short quote), target year, metric and value where stated, subject label, source. Body text, charts and client reports are not stored (see NOTICE.md). Nothing here is graded.

## Verify-first answers

- **Series.** Gartner publishes a "top predictions" list each year, since 2014 presented at Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo in October. The press releases are titled "Gartner Reveals/Unveils Top Predictions for IT Organizations and Users in <year> and Beyond" (2006 to 2010: "Gartner Highlights Key Predictions ..."). The client report is "Gartner's Top Strategic Predictions for <year> and Beyond" (before 2015: "Gartner's Top Predictions for IT Organizations and Users, <year> and Beyond").
- **First edition.** Earliest public trace: a November 2004 research note, "top predictions for 2005 and beyond", known only from a secondary source (2004.json, partial). Earliest Gartner press release found: December 13, 2006 ("2007 and beyond").
- **Public readability.** Press releases list the top predictions in full from 2006 onward. The client reports are paywalled. gartner.com returns 403 (Cloudflare challenge) to curl and WebFetch; all gartner.com releases were read from Internet Archive snapshots (web.archive.org, "id_" raw copies).
- **Edition naming.** One file per year of publication. Calendar 2010 has two editions (January: "2010 and beyond"; November: "2011 and beyond"), so those files are named 2010-01 and 2010-11. Calendar 2005 and 2007 have no edition (see below).

## Counts

- 21 edition files (2004 to 2025). 20 complete, 1 partial (2004).
- 204 top predictions plus 20 near-term flags (2013 and 2014 attach one near-term flag to each prediction; captured as `kind: near_term_flag` with `parent_rank`).
- Where Gartner states a count (10 in every edition from 2014 to 2025, and 2006, 2008, 2009), the found count matches.
- Where Gartner states no count, found counts are 11 (2012), 11 (2011), 8 (2010-11), 9 (2010-01).

## Entry fields

`rank` (order in the release), `kind`, `quote` (headline sentence, max 400 chars), `subject` (plain label, Hindsight's own), `target_year`, `timing` (by / through / in / by the end of / by year-end), `metric`, `value`, `unit`, `source_url`, `confidence`, `note`. `metric`/`value` are null where the prediction has no number.

## Open problems

- **2009** is known only from the Sydney release (February 6, 2009), which leads with one prediction and lists the other nine. No global release found. Confidence stays high because the release states "Top 10 Predictions for 2009" and lists all ten.
- **2006** is read from a verbatim press copy on insurance-canada.ca. The Gartner original URL was not located.
- **2004** is secondary and partial. The "microcommerce" prediction is mentioned but not quoted, so it is not captured.
- **Supporting predictions** inside the body text (2019 blockchain news organizations, 2021 NFT brands, and numbers such as "one billion people affected by digital addiction" in 2024) are not captured as separate entries. Only the headline predictions are.
- **Some headlines are not quantitative** (for example 2023 "productivity value of AI ... primary economic indicator", 2006 "Vista will be the last major release of Microsoft Windows", which has no target year). These carry null metric/value.
- **Revisions (not yet mapped to Subject ids).** Recurring subjects across editions that may be `Revision` candidates: counterfeit reality / fake content (2004, 2017); 3D printing (2008, 2013, 2014); programmable economy and machine customers (2015, 2023, 2025); digital giants (2016, 2017, 2018); energy and electricity (2022, 2023, 2024); smart machines and jobs (2013, 2015, 2017).
