# Envisioning Emerging Technologies (Policy Horizons Canada, 2014)

Envisioning's poster "Envisioning Emerging Technologies". The poster states it was commissioned by Policy Horizon Canada and published in *MetaScan 3: Emerging Technologies*, April 2014. It is a radial technology futures map covering "six key areas" over "the next 15 years": Neurotechnology and cognitive technologies (1-15), Health technologies (16-29), Agricultural technologies (30-44), Nanotechnology and materials science (45-57), Energy technologies (58-74), Digital and communication technologies (75-88). Each sector also has inner sub-areas, such as Storage, Smart grid and Electricity generation. These are named in each entry's note.

Sources: https://www.envisioning.com/work/policy-horizons-canada ; 2014 poster page http://web.archive.org/web/20140719001428/http://www.envisioning.io/horizons/ ; publication https://horizons.gc.ca/en/2014/03/01/metascan-3-emerging-technologies/ (archived 2019-04-09).

Files: `2014.json` (88 placements, not graded), `PROGRESS.md`.

## How time was read

Distance from the centre is time. Thin year lines run from about 2014 at the inner edge of the grid to about 2027 at the outer edge. Thick labelled lines mark 2015, 2020 and 2025. Each technology is a rounded bar with three marks, which the legend defines verbatim:

- Scientifically viable (inner end of the bar): "Concept-stage, unstable, unlikely, sci-fi inspired, billion-dollar budgets. Only Google, governments and universities really invest at this risk level."
- Mainstream point (white numbered dot): "Predicted point when the technology becomes avilable for prototyping in R&D labs, or when VCs and startups start investing in it."
- Financially viable (outer end of the bar): "The point when products and services using the technology are generally available on Kickstarter."

Ring centre (2476.5, 1751.5) px on the 4960x3507 image. Year = 2015 + (r - 695.5) / 32.75 px. This was measured from the ring lines on both axes. Bars and dots were segmented by colour. The dot centre gives `placed_year`/`target_year`. The bar end-cap centres give the `milestones`. All years are rounded to the nearest year. Each dot was checked against an overlay of the computed year rings.

## Grading caveat

On this poster "mainstream" means **R&D prototyping or VC/startup investment**, not mass adoption. It comes before "financially viable", which means products are generally available on Kickstarter. Claims read "<Label> reaches R&D prototyping or venture investment around <year>" (D37). D16's mainstream tests do not apply; the grading rule for this source is pending (D37). The poster also says "Timelines should be interpreted relatively, not literally."
