# IDC FutureScape: collected editions

Facts only: prediction headline (short quote), target year, timing word, metric and value where stated, subject label, source. Report bodies, charts and figures are not stored (see NOTICE.md). Nothing here is graded.

Scope (issue #5): two FutureScapes only, the flagship **Worldwide IT Industry** FutureScape and the **Worldwide CIO Agenda** FutureScape. Each entry carries `futurescape` to say which one.

## Verify-first answers

- **Series.** IDC publishes many FutureScapes a year, one per topic, each with ten predictions. Both in-scope FutureScapes come out in October or early November with a press release and a webinar. The title year is the year after publication ("IDC FutureScape: Worldwide IT Industry 2025 Predictions" came out in October 2024).
- **First edition.** CIO Agenda: earliest one found is the 2015 edition (October 2014, IDC #252235), which calls its ten items "decision imperatives". IDC's text says "As every year", so earlier CIO editions probably exist, but none was found. IT Industry: the first FutureScape-branded edition found is the 2016 edition (November 2015, IDC #259850). Before that, IDC's flagship industry forecast was "IDC Predictions 20XX" (for example "IDC Predictions 2015: Accelerating Innovation — and Growth — on the 3rd Platform", December 2014, IDC #252700; "IDC Predictions 2014: Battles for Dominance — and Survival — on the 3rd Platform", December 2013, IDC #244606). Those are topic lists with many sub-predictions, not "By 20XX, X%" headlines, and are not captured (see open problems).
- **Public readability.** The reports are paywalled. For document-type FutureScapes (up to the 2025 editions) IDC's public table-of-contents page (`research/viewtoc.jsp?containerId=<IDC #>`) lists every prediction headline. Live pages work for 2021 to 2024 publications; older ones come from Internet Archive copies. From the 2026 editions (October 2025) the reports are slide presentations with no public table of contents. Press releases mostly give themes, not the prediction sentences.
- **Edition naming.** One file per publication year (edition "2024" holds the "2025 Predictions" FutureScapes). Both FutureScapes of a year go in the same file. `futurescapes[]` in each file gives title, IDC number, publication date, status and stated versus found counts per FutureScape.

## Counts

- 12 edition files (2014 to 2025). Complete: 2017 to 2024 (8 files, both FutureScapes). Partial: 2025 (CIO missing), 2016 (IT missing), 2015 (CIO partial), 2014 (CIO only; IT precursor not captured).
- 204 entries: 100 IT Industry, 104 CIO Agenda.
- Stated versus found: every FutureScape with a found list states 10 and has 10, except CIO Agenda 2016 (4 found; count not stated in the secondary source). Two FutureScapes (CIO Agenda 2026, IT Industry 2017) state 10 and have 0 found.

## Entry fields

`futurescape`, `rank` (IDC's prediction number; null where the source does not give IDC's order), `idc_label` (IDC's short label, where the headline has one: 2016 IT, 2020 IT hashtags, 2015 CIO), `quote` (headline sentence as written, IDC's title case kept), `subject` (Hindsight's plain label), `target_year`, `timing` (by / through / in / into / by the end of / by early / from 2018 to), `metric`, `value`, `unit`, `source_url`, `confidence`, `note`. `value` keeps IDC's qualifier ("over 50", "at least 25", "nearly 67"). Where a headline holds several numbers, `value` is the first (lead) number and the others are in `note`. `metric`/`value` are null where the headline has no number.

## Realized values

No `realized.json`. The predictions measure shares of CIOs, enterprises or IT budgets that IDC estimates from its own surveys. No public series with a stated source and vintage exists for them. Grading will need a named data source per claim (issue #5 says the same).

## Open problems

- **IT Industry 2017 (edition 2016) missing.** The IDC document number was not found, so the TOC could not be read. The known full list (Forbes, Gil Press, November 1, 2016) returns 403 and has no Internet Archive copy. InformationWeek (https://www.informationweek.com/it-infrastructure/digital-transformation-underway-many-will-fail-at-it) paraphrases the ten predictions; paraphrases are not captured.
- **CIO Agenda 2026 (edition 2025) missing.** The report is a 39-slide presentation. IDC's umbrella release (https://my.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prUS53883425, October 23, 2025) quotes seven predictions from several FutureScapes without saying which one each comes from. One of them ("By 2030, up to 20% of G1000 organizations will have faced lawsuits, substantial fines, and CIO dismissals ...") is probably CIO Agenda, but the attribution is not stated, so it is not captured.
- **CIO Agenda 2018 (edition 2017)** comes from a trade-press copy. It lists ten predictions without numbers; `rank` is null. IDC's own PDF link (idc.com/itexecutive/...) now redirects to a web page.
- **CIO Agenda 2016 (edition 2015)** is partial (4 of 10) from CIO.com; `rank` is null.
- **IT Industry 2020 (edition 2019)** comes from IDC's webinar deck, hosted by a third party. The IDC document number was not found.
- **Pre-FutureScape IT predictions** ("IDC Predictions 2014", "IDC Predictions 2015", and the Frank Gens lists back to about 2008) are topic-headed lists with several numeric sub-predictions under each topic (for example "Overall ICT spending growth will slow modestly to 3.8% in 2015"). They are a different format and outside the FutureScape scope. They could be captured as a separate source if wanted. The 2015 report PDF is at https://vods.dm.ux.sap.com/digitalhub/belux-runsimple/2015/pdfs/downloadasset.2014-12-dec-19-22.idc-predictions-2015-accelerating-innovation--and-growth--on-the-3rd-platform-pdf.bypassReg.pdf.
- **Compound headlines.** Many Frank Gens-era IT headlines (2016, 2018, 2019, 2020 title years) pack two to four numbers and horizons into one sentence. Only the lead number is in `value`; the rest are in `note`. A grader must split them.
- **Headlines without a number** (IT 2023 #10 machine vision, IT 2022 #10 data controls and valuations, IT 2021 #6 cloud ecosystem, IT 2024 #3 and #4) have null metric and value.
- **Repeated text across FutureScapes.** IT Industry 2021 #4 and CIO Agenda 2021 #3 are the same sentence (pandemic technical debt, 70% of CIOs, through 2023).
- **Revisions (not yet mapped to Subject ids).** Recurring subjects across editions that are `Revision` candidates: LEO satellite connectivity (IT 2024: 80% by 2028; IT 2026: 75% by 2029); digitized share of global GDP (IT 2018: at least 50% by 2021; IT 2019: 60%+ by 2022); cloud megaplatform concentration (IT 2019: top 4 host 80% by 2022; IT 2020: top 5 hold at least 75% by 2023); 3rd Platform share of IT spending (IT 2016: over 50% by 2017; IT 2019: 75% by 2023); technical debt (CIO 2017, 2021, 2022, 2025; IT 2021); skills shortfall blocking full value (IT 2023: 65% through 2024; IT 2024: 65% through 2026); low-code developers (IT 2018, IT 2019, CIO 2021, CIO 2024); AI governance (CIO 2019, 2024, 2025).
- **Internet Archive** was offline for part of the session (2026-09-28). CDX listing queries failed; availability lookups worked before and after.
